# Plan: Rewrite the Theory Docs for Outside Readers

**Status: implemented 2026-10-25.** See section 11 for corrections found against the source while
implementing; where sections 4–5 disagree with section 11, section 11 is right.

## Context

`content/docs/` holds 33 articles (~100k words) copied from the compiler's theory docs
(`eco-compiler/design_docs/theory/`). Those were written for engineers joining the project: they are
precise, impersonal specifications that cite invariant numbers and source paths and assume the reader
already knows what monomorphization, MLIR or a statepoint is.

The website audience is different: technically literate people who are curious about Eco — what it is,
how a compiler like it is put together, what optimisations it does, what was tried and measured — some
of whom may go on to contribute. They should come away understanding and interested, not holding a
spec.

The theory docs in the compiler repo remain the single source of truth and the deep reference for
contributors. The website versions become shorter, narrative, example-led articles that point to the
compiler repo for the full detail. They do not have to track every rule; they have to stay true.

Out of scope for this rewrite: a "where help is wanted" / contribution section on articles. That will be
handled separately later.

Paths below starting `eco-compiler/` are relative to `/home/rupert/sc/gitlab/eco-compiler/`.

## Goals

1. A reader can find their level: a short "what and why" first, then a big-picture tour, then optional
   deep dives.
2. Every major article shows at least one concrete before/after example from real compiler output or
   real source.
3. The interesting stories — design decisions, measurements, abandoned designs — are up front, not
   buried.
4. Internal housekeeping, invariant numbers, repo paths and harness mechanics are gone from the body
   text.
5. Total length roughly halves (target ~45–55k words), mostly by merging the test docs and cutting
   internal detail, not by dropping ideas.

## 1. Style guide for the rewrite

Apply to every article.

**Voice and tone**

- Use "we" for the project's decisions and history ("We tried hoisting constants out of functions; it
  made things slower"). Use "Eco" for what the system does.
- Keep the existing virtue: every design choice comes with its reason. Keep sentences plain and exact.
- Allow narrative: what problem came first, what was tried, what was measured, what was kept.

**Article shape** (deep-dive articles)

1. **Hook paragraph** (2–4 sentences): the one interesting idea of the article, in plain terms.
   E.g. GC: "Most generational collectors put a write barrier on every pointer store. Eco's has none,
   because in Elm an old object can never point at a younger one."
2. **Background box** (blockquote, optional): 2–5 one-line definitions of terms the article assumes,
   linking to the glossary.
3. **The idea, built up**: start from a small example and widen. One idea per section.
4. **Example(s)**: real Elm in, real output out (trimmed and labelled as trimmed).
5. **Numbers**, where they exist (see section 5), with their caveats.
6. **"Designs we didn't use"** as a highlighted blockquote callout — at most one alternative per article, in two or three sentences. Tell readers what we did; do not catalogue what we did not do (revised 2026-10-25, see §12).
7. **Further reading**: links to related articles by title, plus one line linking the full theory doc
   and the relevant source directory on GitHub.

**Rules**

- Opening paragraphs must not be dense abstracts listing every fact. Move facts down into the body.
- Section intro pages (Compiler, Backend, Runtime) are 600–1000 words; deep dives 1200–2500.
- Link text is the page title ("[Monomorphization](/docs/monomorphization)"), never `monomorphization.md`.
- Code listings: at most ~30 lines; say "trimmed" when attributes or lines are removed.
- Names of internal functions, files and environment variables appear only when a reader would use
  them (e.g. `eco make --text-mlir`), never as citations.
- `description:` frontmatter is a self-contained one-sentence summary that works as card/SEO text (no
  teasers like "…for two reasons.").

### Introduction

**`why-eco` (new, ~800 words)**
- What Eco is in one paragraph: ordinary Elm, compiled to native executables, shared libraries, Node
  addons — plus Elm's JavaScript output unchanged.
- What you can build that you could not before: CLI tools, servers, embedding Elm in another program.
- Why Elm suits native compilation: immutability, no side effects, whole program known (the three
  guarantees from theory.md).
- Headline facts: 1.58x faster than stock Elm JS across 62 benchmarks; the compiler (~240k lines of Elm)
  compiles itself natively; the runtime is ~100k lines of C++; a concurrent GC with no write barrier.
- Who builds it and the project's age (first commit 2025-11-12, ~2,000 commits).

**`theory` → "Eco at a glance" (~1500 words)**
- Keep: the five governing ideas, each cut to one or two paragraphs, and the overview diagram.
- Cut: thread-count rationale details, the shared-heap rejection detail, packaging minutiae (keep a
  short "Shipping one binary"), the invariants housekeeping, the References block.
- Each part gets a one-paragraph summary linking to its section intro.

**`tour` (new, ~2000 words) — the centrepiece**
Follow one small program through every stage, showing real output at each step. Use the shapes program
from `X/src/Main.elm` (`area` over `Circle | Rect | Square`, `sumTo`, `add`):
1. Elm source.
2. Front end: what type checking records (one expression and its solved type; the idea of
   recording types by expression number).
3. Local optimization: the `case` becomes a decision tree (show the `Decider` shape informally).
4. Monomorphization: the specializations created, by name (`Main_area_$_5`, …).
5. Global optimization: call kinds decided.
6. MLIR: `Main_area_$_5` from `X/out/main.mlir:79-98` (trimmed); `Main_add_$_1` (lines 41-45) as the
   three-line warm-up; `main`'s `eco.construct.list` with `head_unboxed = true`.
7. Lowering: one lowering fixture, `eco-compiler/test/codegen/value_to_heap_cons.mlir` (eco op in,
   `llvm.call @__eco_alloc_inline` + stores out).
8. LLVM, link with runtime + kernels, run.
- Tell readers how to reproduce: `eco make src/Main.elm --text-mlir --output=main.mlir`.
- Uses `overview-pipeline.svg`.

**`optimisations` (new, ~1500 words)**
One entry per optimisation: what it does in one or two sentences, a tiny example where possible, a link
to the deep dive. Group as:
- *Before types are concrete*: decision trees, tail calls to loops, operator rewrites, merging function
  boundaries.
- *Whole program*: monomorphization and unboxing; lambda-set specialization; alias forwarding and
  eta-expansion; inlining and pruning; direct and fast calls.
- *Code generation*: stack aggregates / unboxed tuple returns (`$sret`, `$psplit`), bytes fusion,
  constant folding of top-level values.
- *Backend*: inline bump allocation, fast paths as markers, parallel LLVM partitions.
- *Runtime*: barrier-free generational GC, concurrent marking, parallel minor GC, string/list forms.
- **Built, measured, switched off** — a table with the numbers (section 5): CSE, CAF hoisting and
  dedupe, arity raising, `List.map` template, borrow inference, pre-mono inliner. Add the pull quote:
  across three experiments, cutting allocation did not move wall time but cutting promotion did
  (`E/plans/opt-tier4-parked.md:20`).

**`performance` (new, ~1000 words)** — see section 5 for the figures and caveats.

**`glossary` (new)** — one or two sentences each: ABI, SSA, MLIR, dialect, LLVM, lowering,
monomorphization, specialization, lambda set, closure, partial application (PAP), staging/arity,
boxing/unboxed, kernel, eco thread, mutator, nursery/eden, tenuring/promotion, write barrier,
safepoint, statepoint/stack map, JIT/AOT, fixed point, TLA+, model checking.

### The compiler

**`compiler` (section intro)** — keep the two-halves idea, the cache between them, typed vs erased
paths, and "compute each fact once where it can be computed". Keep the diagram. Cut the step-list
Elm code, layering rules, cache-key detail, harness discussion and the References block.

**`front-end`** — hook: Eco reuses Elm's own front end (via Guida) so error messages are identical;
the one addition is that it *keeps* the types. Example: a small function and the type recorded for an
inner expression. Keep "shaped by running as Elm" briefly (stack-safe loops because the compiler runs
on Node during bootstrap).

**`local-optimization`** — examples:
- Decision tree: `X/src/Case.elm` → `X/out/Case-zero.mlir:27-78`, trimmed to two levels; explain that
  each part of the value is tested at most once. Show the `Decider` type from
  `E/compiler/src/Compiler/AST/TypedOptimized.elm:412-424` informally.
- Tail call → loop: `sumTo` → `scf.while` from `X/out/main.mlir:46-73`.
- Boundary merge: `adder a = \b -> a + b` becomes one 2-argument function (`X/out/Stage-zero.mlir`).

**`monomorphization`** — hook: "a function that works for any type has to become one copy per type,
because native code needs to know how big a value is." Examples:
- `X/src/Poly.elm` → signatures in `X/out/poly-zero.mlir`: `identity2` at String
  (`!eco.value`) and Int (`i64`, unboxed); `add` at `(i64,i64)` and `(f64,f64)`; two `List.map`
  copies. Then show that at default settings `identity2` and `add` vanish (`X/out/poly-default.mlir`).
- `number` staying open: `X/src/Num.elm` → `X/out/Num-zero.mlir:3-16` (one `let n = 30` at both Int and
  Float; `10 + 3` closed to Int; boxing only for `Debug.toString`).
- Explain specialization names (`Module_name_$_N`).
- Keep: "type inference run again per demand", union-find explained simply, "two engines" (the lighter
  engine exists because the JS-hosted bootstrap stage hits V8's heap limit — good story; see
  section 5).

**`lambda-set-specialization`** — hook: in a higher-order language most calls go through a function
value; if the compiler can work out *which* functions can arrive, it can call them directly. Examples:
- Direct call: `X/src/Stage.elm` `dup = twice; dup inc 10` → `X/out/Stage-zero.mlir:46-51`, two
  `eco.call @Stage_inc_$_3`.
- On/off contrast: same call site in `X/out/Lss-zero.mlir:65` (`singleton_fast`) vs
  `X/out/Lss-nolss.mlir:65` (`generic_apply`).
- Stays generic: `List.foldl` over a list of three different lambdas → `segmentation_unknown`
  (`X/out/Lss-zero.mlir:115`).
- Numbers: turning LSS on removed ~1.9 billion generic closure applications from a self-compile
  (2.59B → 0.66B; static-target calls 83.9% → 94.1%), `E/benchmarks/call-stats.md` Run 28.
- Story: the analysis was once 35% of compile time and judged "not paying for itself on the compiler";
  its cost has since roughly halved. Tell this honestly as history, without claiming a current ledger.
- Keep "the paper's two passes vs Eco's demand-driven version" but simplified; keep "Designs Eco does
  not use".

**`global-optimization`** — examples from `X/out/Stage-zero.mlir`: `scale k = let k2 = k*k in \x y -> …`
becomes one 3-argument function; `inc = adder 1` eta-expanded into a direct call; `dup` alias
disappears. Show the four call kinds as a small table (`direct_known_segmentation`,
`singleton_fast`, `generic_apply`, `segmentation_unknown`). Keep the diagram. Move "Written,
measured and off" into a short summary with a link to `optimisations`.

**`mlir-generation`** — hook: by this point everything is decided; generation only reads decisions.
Examples: `Main_area_$_5` (if not already used in the tour, otherwise link there); stack aggregates
from `X/src/Agg2.elm` → `X/out/Agg2.mlir:3-35` (`divMod` returning two registers via `$sret`,
`magnitude` taking split fields via `$psplit`, `eco.make.tuple2` as an SSA value with no heap
allocation) contrasted with the escaping `foldl` accumulator in `X/out/Agg-default.mlir`. Story: the
first escape-analysis pass promoted 2 of 30,910 candidates and was net negative, so the work moved to
`$sret`/`$psplit` (`E/plans/opt-tier1-aggregate-promotion.md:79-81,132-143`).

**`bytes-fusion`** — examples:
- Encoder: `X/src/Bin3.elm` (`encodeRgb`) → `X/out/Bin3.mlir:30-44`: `bf.alloc 3`, cursor, three
  `bf.write.u8`. Check whether the dead `Encoder` construction still in the front-end output is
  removed by the backend before showing it, or trim it with a note.
- Decoder: the count-loop idiom from `E/test/elm-bytes/src/BytesLoopFusionRealOrderTest.elm:30-47` →
  `X/out/Loop.mlir:27-57` (one `bf.require` for all bytes, then a loop of `bf.read.u8`).
- Number: decoder fusion is worth 2.6x on native (`bytes-static` 27 ms vs `bytes-dynamic` 71 ms).
- Be honest that `map2`/`map3` decoders do not fuse today.

**`javascript-output`, `build-driver`** — shorten. Keep "why Eco keeps Elm's JS generator" (bootstrap
and reference behaviour) and the build diagram. Keep the single-collection-before-linking story
(peak memory ~15 GB → ~9.7 GB; the seven extra collection points bought nothing and cost 9%) but check
which figure is current (`E/benchmarks/gc-opt-loop.md` vs `E/benchmarks/fhr-gc-points.md`).

## 11. Corrections found during implementation

Checked against `eco-compiler/` at commit ffbe4d3c. The articles follow these, not the earlier text.

Facts:

- **Chunked lists are on by default** (`list.chunks = True` since commit 2e496894, August 2026); they
  cut cons cells 47.5% at wall-time parity and lowered minor-GC time and peak memory.
- **`$sret` does not return in registers**: in the dialect it is a multi-result function; the
  lowering passes a pointer to a caller stack slot and returns void.
- **Boundary merging** happens in local optimization only when a `let` sits between the lambdas
  (`scale`); `adder a = \b -> ...` gets its two parameters from eta-expansion in global optimization.
- **CSE is two passes**: the post-monomorphization CSE failed its census gate by 40x; the separate
  MLIR-level CSE caused the NaN-sharing miscompile when switched on, and was reverted.
- **Pre-mono inliner** was shipped default-on (2026-09-11) and turned off four days later
  (commit a574065a) once alias forwarding covered its work: 484 bytes for +0.29% generic dispatches.
- **The 12 GB V8 heap cap was the project's own pin**, not a V8 default (Node's default is 2–4 GB). The
  JS-hosted compile peaked at 11.76 GB and timed out; it finished in 31 minutes at 16 GB.
- **preserve_most** was an attempt to speed up generated code's calls into runtime helpers, not
  backend compile time.
- **Backend 641 s → 19.7 s**: 641 s is a pre-loop history row; the measured series runs
  209.9 s → 19.7 s on a 24-core machine (~350 s CPU).
- **Process ids are 64-bit** and never reused; the old 16-bit wrap story is obsolete.
- **"Root operands"** no longer exist in the eco dialect (removed 2026-10-23).
- **Bytes fusion**: fused code is safe across collections (the cursor is rebuilt after any point that
  may collect). Only `unsignedInt8` decoder shapes and the count-loop idiom fuse in 0.2.0; `map3`
  decoders do not. The dead encoder construction *is* removed by the backend; the dead decoder loop
  call is *not*.
- **Closures**: current theory emits only `$cap`; 0.2.0 output still carries `$clo` wrappers.
- **System libraries** are tested on Linux and macOS in CI; Windows is not supported by `eco/system`.

Counts (recounted):

- End-to-end test programs with directives: 1,345 (677 language, 212 system library, 114 stress, rest
  per-package). MLIR fixtures: 449 (357 eco + 92 byte-fusion). Invariants: 417.
- TLA+: 8 collector models; `models.txt` 376 rows; 371 `.cfg` files; 180 rows expect a named
  violation; canary manifest 237 pins.
- Kernel C++ is ~100k lines including vendored code, so no kernel line count is published.

Numbers added beyond section 5 (each sourced in the article's agent report): escape analysis net
negative at ~103 MB / 5.5M extra objects; ~1/3 of 2-tuple construction sites do not escape;
reachability removes ~23% of functions in the self-compile; removing the canonicalizer saved ~0.5 s
with byte-identical output; the pre-link collection drops RSS from ~8 GB to under 1 GB in 0.35 s with
~3 MB live.

Not published because unverifiable: the "LSS cost roughly halved" claim; the kernel-opt "wall follows
retention" lesson; a Bytes slice copy threshold; native-vs-JS compiler speed.
